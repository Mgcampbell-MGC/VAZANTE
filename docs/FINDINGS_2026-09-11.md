First run of the detection layer over the real CVM population: 44 months (2023-01 to 2026-08), 5,376 fund
classes, 131,692 class-months. Two questions were asked, both free to answer: does the break rule actually
detect, and how many executable situations exist. The answers change the design.

Reproduce with `.venv/bin/python scripts/screen.py` and `scripts/backtest_report.py`. The panel builds from
`data/raw` in about a minute.

## 2. The break rule, as specified, does not detect. Two of its four primaries point the wrong way.

The rule was scored against an exogenous label built from the filing record, and then against the
commercially meaningful event: a class whose provision crosses 25% of the carteira and stays there three months.
708 classes (13.2%) reach that state at some point in the window.

**Conjunction rule as written (3 of 4 primaries, 2 consecutive months):** it fired on 0 of 456 classes that went
dark, and on 4 of 4,912 that did not. Loosened to 2 of 4 in a single month it caught 3.3% of dark classes and
5.2% of controls. It is not weak. It is inverted.

**Why, measured per indicator.** Share of class-months firing in the twelve months before a provision blow-up,
against a control window of the same length:

| Indicator | Primary | Fires pre-event | Fires control | Lift | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Provision > 10% of carteira | — | 23.1% | 2.9% | **8.0x** | strongest single signal |
| 180+ bucket > 5% of carteira | — | 16.3% | 5.1% | **3.2x** | works |
| Churn intensity > 5% | — | 7.1% | 2.9% | 2.4x | works |
| 180+ bucket grew > 50% in 12m | D (half) | 13.8% | 6.6% | 2.1x | works |
| Recompra intensity > 3% | A | 8.8% | 4.4% | 2.0x | works, weakly |
| Provision coverage of 90+ < 0.60 | C | 6.9% | 5.5% | 1.25x | near-useless |
| Churn-to-default < 0.35 | B | 1.2% | 1.4% | **0.81x** | INVERTED |
| Senior return 12m stdev < 0.15pp | D (half) | 19.0% | 29.5% | **0.64x** | INVERTED |

**The smooth senior return is the most important correction.** The business case makes it one of the three
sentences that buy exclusivity: *"your senior class has returned CDI + N% to eight decimals for nineteen months
while your 180-plus bucket tripled."* Measured, a flat senior return is more common in healthy funds than in
breaking ones, which on reflection is obvious: a performing CDI-plus fund with adequate subordination is exactly
what produces a smooth series. Only the second half of that sentence, the growing 180+ bucket, carries signal.
Said as written to a fund picked by this tell, GC would be describing a healthy fund to its own manager.

**Churn-to-default is inverted for the same kind of reason.** Its denominator is repurchases plus substitutions,
which is only computable on the 10% to 20% of classes that report any. Among those, a low ratio marks an
actively revolving healthy book, not a suppressed one.

**What survives.** A rule built only from ageing and churn, using no provision term so it cannot be tautological,
separates the two groups at 3.2x on a class-month basis. At class level with twelve months of forward visibility
it reaches 7.9% precision against a 4.4% base rate, a lift of 1.8x, at 39% recall. That is real but modest. It
is a ranking tool, not a trigger.

**Consequence.** The conjunction rule must be refitted before it drives a calling list, and the thresholds in
`config/thresholds.yaml` stay tagged ASSUMPTION until it is. The Day-1 sheet's field 8 needs rewriting: drop the
quota sentence, keep the recompra sentence, and lead with the provision and the 180+ bucket. This is a design
change and it needs a partner's sign-off, so nothing has been altered in the thresholds file yet.

## 3. The orphaned vehicles are not the natural attack. The data is unambiguous.

The question was whether the funds orphaned by a liquidated administrator are where to point the desk. They are
the largest and most visible pool, and they fail on paper type and on size.

| Lineage | Classes still filing | PL | Median PL | Median industrial+comercial share | Pass loss-booked | Pass shape | **Pass size** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Trustee | 38 | R$9.5bn | R$25.9m | **0.0%** | 5 | 0 | **0** |
| Banvox | 32 | R$5.2bn | R$15.1m | **0.0%** | 8 | 0 | **0** |
| CBSF | 59 | R$48.1bn | R$53.6m | **0.0%** | 8 | 0 | **0** |
| Sefer | 24 | R$7.9bn | R$50.0m | **0.0%** | 9 | 1 | **0** |
| Master | 8 | R$6.7bn | R$19.3m | 100.0% | 2 | 2 | **0** |

Not one of the 161 classes across all five lineages passes the size gate. Two separate reasons, and both are
fatal on their own. Their paper is mostly not multicedente commercial duplicata at all: the median
industrial-plus-commercial share is zero for four of the five lineages, meaning the carteiras are financial,
consignado or other segments the desk cannot decompose or resell. And they are small: median PL between R$15m
and R$54m against a R$85m floor. Master is the exception on paper type, with 100% industrial and commercial, and
it is the smallest of the five at R$19.3m median.

The attrition is real. Master went from 74 classes filing in 2025-01 to 10 in 2026-01. CBSF fell from 236 in
2025-07 to 59 in 2026-07. Trustee and Banvox roughly halved over 2026. So the vehicles are genuinely leaving,
but what is leaving is not what the desk buys.

**Archetype B, the funds already in liquidação, is also a dead end at size.** 196 FIDC classes sit in
"Em Liquidação" carrying R$7.0bn. 32 have the loss booked. Five have the loss booked and the right paper. All
five carry PL between minus R$3m and R$9m, with provisions of 500% to 200,000% of a carteira already written to
almost nothing. There is nothing left to buy.

**A framing note.** The counts in the question, phrased as mandates, laudo and mesa revenue against R$4bn under
servicing, belong to the fee model the business case killed. Under the principal model the question is not how
many vehicles need a home. It is how many carteiras are the right paper, at the right size, with a signer who
can sell and is willing to book the loss. Those are different populations, and the orphan pool scores well on
the first question and badly on the second.

## 4. The executable count is 18, and the business case's estimate of about 14 was close

Funnel over the 4,321 FIDC classes filing at 2026-07:

| Gate | Classes | PL |
|---|---|---|
| FIDC classes filing | 4,321 | R$1,015bn |
| Loss already booked, provision >= 25% of carteira | 420 | R$58.0bn |
| Right paper, industrial + commercial >= 60% | 99 | R$14.6bn |
| Carteira >= R$60m documented-face floor | 21 | R$13.1bn |
| PL >= R$85m | 18 | R$13.0bn |
| Gestor present and distinct from the administrador | 16 | — |

Sixteen named classes, saved to `data/derived/calling_list_202607.csv`. The business case's central estimate of
roughly fourteen executable situations was ASSUMPTION and is now VERIFIED at 18 before the willingness test and
16 after the authority test. That is the closest any headline number in the design has come to surviving contact
with the data.

Two cautions on the list. It has not been filtered for willingness, which the business case says removes about
70% of the pool, so expect it to fall to about five. And the redemption-suspension filter, the art. 44 §3
trigger that is the single best willingness proxy, is not in it: it needs Fundos.NET, which is not built.

The list lands nowhere near the orphans. Its administrators are Hemera, BTG Pactual, Limine Trust, QI, Qore,
Finaxis, Genial and Vórtx, all in good standing. Three of the sixteen are managed by Polígono Capital, which is
a name Sutphin already knows.

One entry is a data anomaly worth a look before anyone calls: a fund reporting a provision at 444% of a carteira
that has collapsed to R$24m from R$179m of PL. Either the write-down is near total or the filing is wrong, and
which of those it is decides whether it is an opportunity or noise.

## 5. Source corrections

- **`cad_fi.csv` is the wrong register for FIDCs.** 46,575 of its 46,806 rows are CANCELADA, only 22 are in
  normal operation, and just 1.6% of live informe classes match it. It is the pre-Res. CVM 175 legacy file. The
  live register is `FI/CAD/DADOS/registro_fundo_classe.zip`, which matches 98.7% of classes and carries gestor,
  diretor responsável, class situação and the Entidade_Investimento flag that the captive-FIDC tax gate turns on.
- **CNPJ formats differ between sources.** The informe punctuates, the register does not. Join on digits only.
- **Res. CVM 175 moved the informe from fund level to class level during 2024.** Before that the X tables key on
  `CNPJ_FUNDO`; after, on `CNPJ_FUNDO_CLASSE`. Tabela X, the SCR risk buckets, does not exist before 2024.
- **History lives in yearly archives** under `INF_MENSAL/DADOS/HIST/`, back to 2013, holding CSVs directly
  rather than nested monthly ZIPs. Only the current and prior year are published as monthly files.
- **`alienações ao cedente` is unusable.** The field is populated on 0.2% of class-months, so the secondary
  signal "alienações ao cedente exceeding alienações a terceiros" cannot be computed on this population.

## 6. What this changes

1. **The detection edge is real but weaker and differently shaped than designed.** The provision and the far
   ageing bucket carry the signal at 3x to 8x lift. The quota-smoothness tell is backwards and must come out of
   the script. Refit before the rule drives anything.
2. **Point the desk at live administrators, not liquidated ones.** The orphan cohort fails on paper type and
   size, measurably and without exception.
3. **The size floor is the binding constraint, not supply.** 99 classes have the loss booked and the right
   paper; only 21 clear R$60m of carteira. Lowering the floor is the single biggest lever on deal count, and the
   business case already shows what it costs: at R$35m of face the trade earns R$0.94m after tax and fails the
   hurdle. That tension is now measured rather than assumed.
4. **Willingness remains the untested filter and is still the whole game.** The machine can rank 4,321 classes
   in a minute. It cannot tell you which signer will crystallise a loss. That needs the twelve written questions,
   and nothing in the data will answer it.
