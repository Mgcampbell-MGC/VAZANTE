# Prompt for a website-design LLM

**13 September 2026. A draft. Nothing goes live without a partner's signature.**

Paste everything between the rules below into a website-design LLM (v0, Lovable, Claude, Figma Make).
It is written to be handed over whole — it carries the business, the reader, the argument, the
prohibitions and the visual direction, so the designer does not have to guess.

## Two decisions to make before you send it

**One. How much of the screen do we give away?** The single most persuasive fact we have is that these
books have no disposal channel — third-party sales of R$5.0m ever, nothing in twenty-two months, against
R$1,033m a year of cedente repurchase. Put that on a public page and every competitor learns what we
found, and the moat is two to four quarters wide at most. **The prompt below states the argument without
publishing the screen**: it says what is true of broken multicedente books in general, and keeps the
twenty-five names, the R$0 and the R$1,033m for the call. If MGC wants the harder version, the fact goes
in and the advantage goes with it.

**Two. Who signs the page.** A seller-facing site is external text under operating rule 1. The prompt
tells the designer to leave the team, contact and legal-entity details as fields for a partner to fill,
rather than inventing them.

# THE PROMPT — copy from here

You are designing the public website for **VAZANTE**, a São Paulo credit desk. Design and build it.

## Who this is for

**The reader is a gestor or an administrador of a Brazilian FIDC that has gone wrong.** He is a
professional, he is regulated, and he is not a retail visitor. He has a fund whose provision has climbed
for two years, whose subordinada is impaired, whose cotista asks a question every month he cannot answer
well. He has probably been approached before by people who wanted to buy the whole book at a number that
insulted him, or by advisers who wanted a fee for a document.

**He is sceptical, he is short of time, and he is protecting his own reputation.** He is not looking for
a miracle. He is looking for somebody who has clearly already done the work and is not going to waste
his afternoon.

He will read the page in Portuguese, on a laptop, probably between meetings, probably after somebody
sent him the link. He will give it ninety seconds.

## The one job of this site

**He should finish it thinking: these people already understand my problem better than the last four who
called, and the thing they are asking for costs me almost nothing to give.**

Not "book a demo". Not "learn more". The conversion is: *he sends the position-level file, or he takes
the call.*

## What VAZANTE actually does — get this exactly right

VAZANTE is **paid by the seller to sell a broken carteira in pieces**.

The insight the whole business rests on, and the site has to make it land in one screen:

**A buyer shown a whole broken carteira has to price the worst line in it.** So he prices the worst line,
the seller refuses, and nothing trades. That is why these books sit. The book does not have a buyer.
**Its pieces do.**

So VAZANTE cuts the carteira along the boundaries that decide who can bid — paper that is not yet due
versus paper long overdue; paper where the cedente is still on the hook versus paper sold outright; the
cedentes who have failed versus the ones still trading — and takes each piece to the buyers who want that
exact risk. Then it runs a competitive round with a deadline and hands the seller a comparison of what
came back.

**Four things about the structure that matter to the reader, and each deserves its own beat:**

1. **VAZANTE never buys.** It is not a counterparty, there is no conflict about the price, and it is not
   making a bid dressed up as a service.
2. **VAZANTE never touches the money.** The buyer pays the fund directly. VAZANTE invoices separately.
3. **The contract is with the management company, not with the fund.** No change to the regulamento, no
   assembleia de cotistas, no fee at fund level, no filing. One director signs.
4. **The ask is small.** One file. Not a mandate, not a meeting, not a commitment.

## The argument, in the order it should unfold

Write the page as one argument, not as a features list. Roughly:

**Open with the problem, stated as his problem, not ours.** Something close to: *a carteira quebrada não
tem comprador. Os pedaços dela têm.* The hero should be that sentence and its consequence, not a company
tagline.

**Then why nothing trades.** The bidder has to price the worst line in the book, so he does. This is the
part that makes him nod, because he has watched it happen.

**Then the cut.** Show it. A book divided into its real pieces, each piece labelled with the kind of firm
that buys it — a fomento house that underwrites the originator, a distressed fund that prices a recovery
curve, the cedente himself under his own recourse clause. **A diagram earns its place here** and it is
probably the single most important visual on the site. It should look like a book being divided, not like
a generic three-column feature row.

**Then the process, with a clock on it.** What happens in week one, week two, week three. Concrete and
short. He is judging whether these people have done this before.

**Then what VAZANTE is not.** Say it plainly — not a buyer, not a counterparty, does not hold the money,
does not act for the fund. Stating the limits builds more trust with this reader than claiming breadth.

**Then how VAZANTE gets paid,** in structure only and with no numbers: a fee to mobilise and build the
lots, a fee when the comparison of offers is delivered, and a share of the result — with the first two
credited against the last, so that if the book sells they cost nothing. **Do not put a single figure on
the page.**

**Close with the ask.** One file, position by position. A short form, or an address. Nothing else.

## Voice

**Portuguese (pt-BR) is the primary language.** Offer an English toggle; do not make English the default.

Write like a principal, not like a marketing department. Short sentences. Concrete nouns. No "solutions",
no "unlock", no "leverage", no "transform", no "journey", no exclamation marks, no emoji. Where a
Brazilian market term is the right word, use it — *carteira*, *cedente*, *sacado*, *coobrigação*,
*inadimplência*, *corretagem*, *informe mensal* — and do not explain them, because explaining them tells
the reader the page was not written for him.

Say *nós* rarely and *você* often. The page is about his fund, not about our firm.

## Prohibitions — legal, and not negotiable

These are regulatory perimeter, not taste. A single breach makes the page a problem.

**Never use these words anywhere on the site, in any language:** *laudo*, *parecer*, *auditoria*,
*avaliação*, *mandato*, *opinião de valor*, *valuation*, *appraisal*, *audit*, *due diligence report*.
VAZANTE forms no opinion of value and issues no report. The contract is a **corretagem** under arts.
722–729 do Código Civil, which introduces without binding; a *mandato* binds and we must not have one.

**Never put a price, a percentage, a fee, a range, a discount, or a "typical recovery" anywhere.** Not in
a table, not in an example, not in fine print.

**Never suggest VAZANTE gives investment advice, manages portfolios, distributes or opines on cotas, or
is authorised by the CVM or the Banco Central.** VAZANTE deals in *direitos creditórios*, which are not
securities. It never touches the cotas. If the page mentions cotas at all, it is only to say it does not
deal in them.

**Never imply anyone committed fraud, nor that a fund or a manager is failing.** The page describes a
market condition, never a named or implied wrongdoer.

**Never name a fund, a manager, an administrator, a cedente or a buyer.** No logos, no client list, no
"trusted by". Nobody has been contacted and nothing is public.

**No fabricated proof.** No invented track record, no made-up deal count, no fake testimonials, no
statistics we have not sourced. Where a number would normally go, use the structure of the argument
instead. If you want a factual anchor, this one is safe and true: **a Brazilian FIDC reports its own
carteira, ageing, provision and monthly movements to the CVM every month, and those filings are public**
— so the page can say that VAZANTE reads them before it calls, which is both true and quietly impressive.

Leave the following as clearly marked placeholders for a partner to fill: legal entity name and CNPJ,
address, the team, the contact address, the privacy notice, and anything in the footer.

## Visual direction

The reader's world is Brazilian credit — monthly filings, ledgers, ageing ladders, notarial documents.
Draw on that rather than on fintech.

**Avoid the current defaults**: no purple-to-blue gradient hero, no near-black page with one acid-green
accent, no warm-cream-and-terracotta serif look, no stock photography of handshakes or skylines, no
floating glass cards, no emoji as section markers, no badge saying "AI-powered".

**Aim for the feel of a serious document rather than a product page.** Considered typography doing most
of the work: one display face with real character for the argument's headlines, one highly legible face
for the body, one monospace for figures and labels. Generous white space. Rules and spacing to separate
sections instead of a card around everything. A restrained palette — a deep, slightly cool ink; a warm
paper ground; one accent used sparingly, and semantic colour kept separate from it.

Motion should be almost absent. One considered transition on the diagram, nothing else.

## Technical requirements

- Single-page site, responsive, correct down to 375px with no horizontal scroll.
- Works in light and dark; respect the visitor's system setting and give both the same care.
- Accessible: real heading hierarchy, visible focus states, AA contrast, `prefers-reduced-motion`
  honoured, all text selectable.
- Fast: no heavy framework needed. System or Google fonts with real fallback stacks.
- `lang="pt-BR"`, correct meta description and Open Graph tags, and a `<title>` that is a name rather than
  a sentence.
- Every form field labelled; the file-submission path must say plainly what happens to the file and that
  it is treated as confidential.

## What done looks like

A gestor with a broken fund reads it in ninety seconds and thinks: *these people have already looked at
my filings, they are not trying to buy my book, they want one file, and there is no reason not to send
it.*

If the page reads like it was written by a marketing team, it has failed. If it reads like it was written
by somebody who has taken a carteira apart before, it has worked.
