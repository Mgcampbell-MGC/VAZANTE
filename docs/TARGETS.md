# The funds we can go after

11 September 2026. Derived from all 4,321 FIDC classes filing with the CVM at competência 2026-07, with every
cedente CNPJ resolved through the Receita. Reproduce with `.venv/bin/python scripts/screen.py`; the full set is
`data/derived/target_universe.csv`, cedente identities in `data/derived/cedentes_resolved.json`.

**Nothing here has been contacted, and nothing here is a price.** This is desk intelligence. The willingness
filter, redemption suspension under art. 44 §3, needs Fundos.NET and is not built, so this is a research list.

---

## The shape of the answer

Only two funds in the whole market are both the right paper and big enough to carry a VAZANTE trade on their
own. Below them sits a set of genuinely broken multicedente books that are the right shape and too small alone,
but which cluster under a handful of gestoras, so one conversation reaches several at once.

That gives three ways in, and the business case needs all three: a tiny Deal 1 to calibrate execution, a real
Deal 2, and a route that does not run out after two names.

---

## Tier 1 — big enough to be a trade on its own

### 1. DEL CRED NP FIDC MULTISSETORIAL
`11.468.192/0001-81` · carteira **R$344m** · PL R$357m · provision **37.8%**, up 17.0 points in twelve months

Administrador Limine Trust. Gestor **Tercon Investimentos**. Diretor Eduardo Pereira Rocha. Three quota-holders
across two classes. Não-padronizado and multissetorial, 65% business-to-business paper, 19.4% of the carteira
more than 180 days overdue.

The largest genuinely-shaped book in the market. Two cautions. Limine names no cedentes at all, so what is
inside is unknown from outside and only the tape will tell. And the carteira **grew 39% over 24 months** while
the provision rose from 15.2% to 37.8%, which means the manager is still feeding it. A fund still being fed is a
fund whose manager has not given up, and that is the hardest willingness case in the business case's table.

Next check: the regulamento and the last two demonstrações contábeis on Fundos.NET. Whether the auditor has put
a ressalva on the lastro decides whether this is a conversation or a dead end.

### 2. PONTUAL BRASIL FIDC MULTISSETORIAL
`31.844.721/0001-01` · carteira **R$170m** · PL R$170m · provision **46.8%**, up 17.2 points

Administrador QI Corretora. Gestor **Ouro Preto Gestão de Recursos**. Diretor Daniel Doll Lemos. **Two
quota-holders.** 64% business-to-business, 17.6% beyond 180 days. Carteira flat over 24 months while the
provision went from 12.9% to 46.8%, so this book has stopped growing and started being written down.

Three cedentes are named, and the mix is the pathology the desk exists for:

| Cedente | What it is | Capital | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Aluminum Brasil S.A. | cable and conductor maker, Guarulhos | R$200,000 | active |
| Furnax Comercial e Importadora | industrial machinery wholesaler, Itapevi | R$1.2m | active |
| Dioxyl Revestimentos Químicos | paint and varnish maker, Santa Catarina | R$151,515 | **in judicial recovery** |

Unrelated industries, unrelated regions, one in recovery, and capital of a few hundred thousand reais standing
behind millions of face. That is a real multicedente book, not a captive one.

**This is the best single name on the list.** Two quota-holders means one institution can decide. A named
cedente already in judicial recovery means the Claims strip is not hypothetical, and the December 2025 STJ
ruling that judicial recovery does not suspend action against guarantors is what makes that strip worth paying
for.

## Tier 2 — the clusters, where one conversation reaches several books

The broken books concentrate. This matters more than any individual name, because it solves the size problem: no
single Ouro Preto vehicle except Pontual clears the floor, but the three together are R$207m. And a gestora
holding several broken books of one kind is the exiting-gestora archetype, which the business case calls the
highest-willingness signature there is. It has one decision to make, not three.

| Gestora | Books | Combined carteira | The vehicles |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ouro Preto Gestão** | 3 | **R$207m** | Pontual R$170m · Albatroz R$21m · Health Mercantil R$16m |
| **Tercon Investimentos** | 5 | **R$378m** | Del Cred R$344m · Target R$34m · plus Jeitto, BJ and Sercard written to zero |
| **Libertas Asset** | 2 | R$60m | Prosper R$41m · Milagre R$20m |
| **Kanastra** | 2 | R$99m | Medsystems B24 II R$68m · Medsystems B24 R$32m (one sponsor, so captive) |
| **Tueri Gestora** | 2 | R$24m | AGA R$18m · Canada Invest R$5m |

By administrador the same concentration appears: **Limine Trust** administers six of the qualifying books worth
R$428m (Del Cred, Albatroz, AGA, Health Mercantil, Factia, D'Oro), all under different gestoras. Limine is one
conversation that touches six vehicles and five managers.

Ouro Preto and Tercon are the two calls worth making. Tercon is the larger pool and three of its five are
already written to nothing, which is the "signer has no NAV to protect" condition in its purest form.

## Tier 3 — the right shape, too small alone, and exactly what Deal 1 should be

The business case asks for a first trade at a purchase price of R$1.5m to R$3m, small enough that losing all of
it costs less than the tax opinion. These are that trade. Each is a genuine multicedente book, deeply
provisioned, with named cedentes in real industries.

## What to do with this, in order

1. **Pull the Fundos.NET file on Pontual and Del Cred.** Regulamento for the disposal-authority clause and the
   coobrigação language, the last two demonstrações contábeis for the auditor's opinion on the lastro, and
   twelve months of atas and fatos relevantes for any redemption suspension. This is the missing filter and it
   is free.
2. **Decide whether the first call is a fund or a gestora.** The data says gestora. Ouro Preto holds Pontual
   plus two more; Tercon holds Del Cred plus four. One conversation, several vehicles, and the exiting-gestora
   archetype is the highest-willingness case in the design.
3. **Price Daniele I as the Deal 1 candidate.** It is the right shape, the right size for the tiny first trade,
   and it has four cedentes in judicial recovery, two of them substantial, so it exercises the Claims strip that
   nobody has ever tested.
4. **Resolve the quota-holders.** Pontual has two, Milagre and Factia and AGA have one each. A single
   institutional quota-holder is archetype D, and the business case's strongest unproven idea is to ask that
   holder to take the paper in kind and then bid on its asset. One quota-holder makes that a single conversation.

## What is still missing, and it is not small

**Willingness is untested and it is the whole game.** Every name here passes on paper, size and provision.
Nothing in the CVM data says whether the signer will crystallise a loss. The business case expects that filter
to remove about 70% of the pool, which would take two Tier 1 names down to roughly one.

**Del Cred is dark on cedentes.** Limine names none, so the largest target's contents are unknown until a tape
arrives.

**The senior quota return is useless in both directions.** The business case uses a suspiciously smooth senior
return as one of the three sentences that buy exclusivity. Measured, a smooth return fires on 19.0% of breaking
funds and 29.5% of healthy ones. The opposite reading, a return that has stopped, is no better: 42.2% against
55.3%. Adding it to a detection score makes the score worse. Drop the field from the method and from the script.
