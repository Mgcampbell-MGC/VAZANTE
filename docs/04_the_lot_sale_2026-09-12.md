# The Lot Sale

**12 September 2026. This replaces the business model in Part 3 of the business case. The screen, the data
and the target list all survive unchanged. What changes is who pays us and what we sell.**

---

## The model in one paragraph

We go to the gestora of a broken fund and say: **give us your tape.** We cut the book into lots. Each lot
goes to the buyer who already wants that exact risk. The gestora pays us to run the process and pays us
again out of what we beat.

**VAZANTE never buys.** No capital. No title risk. No tax opinion. No settlement. We hold no money at any
point — the buyer pays the fund directly and we invoice separately.

---

## Why this is faster than buying

The February model needed six things before a single real moved: cash on the balance sheet, a registradora
contract, a tax opinion on the factoring trap, per-cedente assignment mechanics, notice to every debtor, and
three firm exits covering 1.20× before we could bid.

**This model needs one signature.**

We contract with the **gestora as a company**, not with the fund. That keeps us outside Anexo II of
Resolução CVM 175 completely. No change to the regulamento. No assembly of quota-holders. No fund-level fee
disclosure. No CVM anything. One director signs and we start.

## The fact that sells this

Across all twenty-five target funds, for the whole reporting period, the CVM Informe Mensal reports:

| | |
|---|---|
| **Sales to third parties** | **R$0** |
| Cedente contractual buybacks | R$57m |

**VERIFIED.** From their own filings. Not one real of this paper has ever been sold to anybody.

These funds have no disposal channel. The only money leaving them is the automatic repurchase the cedente
was already contractually obliged to make. Nobody has negotiated a sale of anything to anyone.

That is not a pitch we invented. It is their own number, and we can put it in front of them.

## Why nothing trades today

A buyer shown a whole broken carteira has to price the worst line in it.

So he prices the worst line. The seller refuses. Nothing happens. That is the R$0.

**The book does not have a buyer. Its pieces do.**

## The four lot boundaries

We draw the line where **the buyer's model breaks**, not where the seller's book breaks. There are four, and
three of them are free.

**One. Recourse.** Paper with coobrigação is credit risk on the originator. Paper without it is credit risk
on the debtor. Those are two different products bought by two different kinds of firm. Putting them in one
lot forces every bidder to underwrite both. Splitting them doubles the bidder pool at zero cost.

**Two. Age.** Not-yet-due, under 90, 90 to 180, over 180. Each bucket has its own pricing method. A fund that
prices at 8 centavos does not want the performing block and cannot pay for it.

**Three. Cedente status.** Nine of the sixty-four named cedentes are in judicial recovery. Where that paper
carried recourse, the fund holds a claim in that recovery. Claims trade in a completely different market.

**Four. Size.** This is the one that decides speed, and it is covered below.

---

## The speed number

**Fifty-four lots, across twenty-one of the twenty-five funds, are under R$10m of face.**

R$156m of face in total. **MODEL** — computed by `scripts/lot_plan.py` from the informe buckets.

At a distressed price that is a cheque between R$300k and R$3m. Every serious buyer on our list has a
founder or a partner who signs that alone.

**Over R$10m the deal goes to a committee, and a committee is six weeks.** Under it, one person decides.

So the speed of this business is not a function of how fast we work. It is a function of how we cut. Cut
small and the answer comes back in days from one person. Cut big and we wait for a Tuesday meeting.

## What makes a buyer say yes immediately

Five things. A lot that has all five gets an answer in the room.

**One risk in the lot.** He prices it with the model he already has, without adjustment. The moment a lot
contains two kinds of risk he has to build something, and building something means next month.

**Under his own signature limit.** See above. This is the single most controllable variable we have.

**Not already round-tripped.** In a market where every visible book has been through four brokers, an
unshopped book is the scarcest thing on offer. We cannot prove nobody has ever called these funds — we only
know that *we* have not. What we can prove is stronger and it is the R$0: whoever may have called, nothing
was ever sold. **VERIFIED.** That is the line to use with a buyer, because it is checkable and the other
one is not.

**The file is already built.** He does not do the work. Cedentes resolved through the Receita, judicial
recovery flagged with dates, ageing already cut, documentation inventoried. He reads and decides.

**A date.** The round closes on a stated day. Without one, every buyer's correct move is to wait.

## How we get paid

Three lines, one contract.

**Line 1 — mobilisation.** Paid on signature, due in five days. This buys the lot structure and the first
round. It is fully credited against the success fee, so **if the book sells it costs them nothing.** Say
that in the first sentence.

**Line 2 — the round fee.** Paid when we deliver the comparative bid tape: written, dated indications on one
standard form from at least three named buyers. **This is the line that decouples our cash from their
closing.** We get paid for producing a market whether or not they like the price it produces.

**Line 3 — success, and this is where the high fee lives.**

A percentage of proceeds on a distressed sale is small money, because the proceeds are small. R$371m of
deep paper at eight centavos is R$30m of proceeds, and three percent of that is R$890k. Real, but not high.

**The high fee is the upside share.** Before round one, the gestora states a reserve in writing — the number
he expects. We take a percentage of everything above it.

That works here and nowhere else, because of the R$0. These funds have never sold anything, so their
internal mark is a provisioned book value nobody has ever tested against a bid. The reserve they state will
be the number they have privately given up on. Beating it is not hard. And they pay the upside share only on
money they told us in writing they did not expect to see.

**It is uncapped, it is defensible, and it is the only structure in which "high" survives the conversation.**

## The legal shape

**Contrato de corretagem, Código Civil arts. 722 a 729.** A broker not bound to the other party by mandate
or employment, who obtains business for it according to instructions received, and is paid on result.

Deliberately **not** a *mandato*. A mandato under arts. 653 and following carries the power to bind the
principal, and we must not have it. We introduce; the gestora signs.

The object is **direitos creditórios**, which are absent from the list of valores mobiliários in Lei 6.385
art. 2. Brokering them needs no licence.

The bright line to stay on the right side of: **carteira, never cotas.** Cotas de FIDC *are* securities.
We sell the assets and never express a view on anybody's cotas.

Four things the contract must say, and they are short:

1. VAZANTE **aproxima partes**. It renders no assessoria de investimento, no consultoria de valores
   mobiliários, no auditoria, no avaliação and no parecer, and **emits no opinion of value**.
2. **VAZANTE never receives the proceeds.** The buyer pays the fund directly. This is the cleanest single
   line in the document and it is what keeps us outside Lei 4.595 art. 17.
3. The seller's own counsel papers the cessão. VAZANTE drafts nothing.
4. Our buyer relationships are disclosed. CC art. 723 puts a duty of diligence and information on the
   broker; we satisfy it by saying so up front rather than being asked.

**The vocabulary rule survives intact.** Never *laudo*, never *parecer*, never *auditoria*, never an opinion
of value — not in the contract, not in the sale book, not in an email. That rule was never about the
business model. It is about not being characterised as something that needs a licence.

## What changes in the repository rules

`CLAUDE.md` rule 3 currently reads *"Never a fee, a mandate or a report. VAZANTE buys and sells; it renders
no service."* That was written for the principal desk.

**MGC has now decided otherwise, and the rule follows the decision.** VAZANTE renders a service and is paid
a fee for it. `CLAUDE.md` is updated accordingly in the same commit as this document.

What does **not** change: no price to a seller out of a session, the forbidden vocabulary, no fund-level
fraud score, numbers computed and tagged rather than typed, evidence through `pull()`, and nothing from
`data/` in git.

## The three things that could kill this

**One. "Bring me a bid first and then we will talk about a fee."**

This is what a fiduciary with no budget line says, and it is the most likely single sentence we will hear.
It is worse than a no, because it feels like progress. It converts a paid engagement into a contingent one
and produces nothing for four months.

The defence is price. A fee small enough not to be a decision gets one director's signature. A fee large
enough to need a committee is four weeks gone. And it must be fully creditable, stated in the first
sentence, so that the honest summary is: *if it sells, this costs you nothing.*

**Two. The performing block may not be performing.**

R$1,300m of face — 63.5% of everything — is reported as not yet due and never missed, inside funds
provisioning at an average 49.5%. Those two facts are hard to hold together. Either these books are
massively over-provisioned, which makes Lot A the best thing on the table, or the provision is telling us
about paper that is about to migrate, which makes Lot A nearly worthless.

**We do not know which, and it is the largest open question in the business.** Only a tape answers it. Until
one arrives, nothing about Lot A should be said to a buyer.

**Three. We may be a good broker instead of a desk.**

Brokerage pays sooner and costs nothing to run, and revenue shapes what a company becomes. The risk is not
that this fails. It is that it works, comfortably, and the principal trade never happens because nobody
needs it to.

If that is the outcome MGC wants, it should be chosen rather than drifted into.
